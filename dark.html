Journalist's Guide to the Dark Web

Secure research methods for investigative reporting

Important Warning: The dark web contains both valuable resources and significant risks. Journalists should proceed with caution, follow all applicable laws, and consult with their organization's security team before beginning dark web research.

Understanding the Dark Web

The dark web is a small portion of the deep web that requires special software to access. For journalists, it offers:

  • Secure communication with whistleblowers and sources
  • Access to information from censored regions
  • Alternative perspectives on news events
  • Research into cybercrime and illicit markets (for investigative purposes)

Essential Safety Precautions

Journalistic Best Practice: Always maintain a separate research device or virtual machine for dark web activities to avoid compromising your primary work environment.
  • Never use personal information or credentials
  • Assume all activity could be monitored by adversaries
  • Verify all information through multiple sources
  • Understand your legal protections and limitations

Step-by-Step Access Guide

1

Install the Tor Browser

Download from the official Tor Project website: torproject.org

Always verify the download's PGP signature (instructions provided on Tor site).

2

Configure Security Settings

After installation:

  • Click the security settings (shield icon)
  • Set to "Safest" level for maximum protection
  • Disable JavaScript (can be re-enabled temporarily for specific sites)
  • Consider using a VPN before launching Tor (consult your IT team)
3

Understand Onion Addresses

Dark web sites use .onion addresses (e.g., protonmailrmez3lotccipshtkleegetolb73fuirgj7r4o4vfu7ozyd.onion).

These are only accessible through Tor and provide end-to-end encryption.

Journalistic Resources

SecureDrop

Whistleblower submission system used by major news organizations. Find your outlet's SecureDrop portal or explore the directory.

Dark Web News Outlets

Many mainstream organizations have onion versions:

  • BBC Tor Mirror
  • ProPublica
  • The New York Times

Verified Directories

Reliable starting points:

  • Dark.fail (verifies official URLs)
  • Dread (forum with journalist sections)

Ethical Guidelines

  • Focus on information in the public interest, not sensationalism
  • Never participate in or facilitate illegal activities
  • Protect vulnerable sources while verifying their information
  • Consider the potential consequences of your reporting
  • Maintain professional detachment
Remember: The dark web is a research tool, not a story in itself. Your journalism should focus on verified information and its public value, not the method of discovery.
